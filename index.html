What the Kirby-Desai Scale Cannot Know

The Kirby-Desai scale provides a statistical reference range based on a 100-patient retrospective study. It is not an individual guarantee. Four clinical variables cannot be measured by a questionnaire:

1. Individual Immune & Lymphatic Phagocytosis

Lasers shatter ink particles; your body's macrophages and lymphatic system actually clear the ink. Individual macrophage activity and metabolic clearance speed vary widely.

2. Unregulated Ink Chemistry & Pigment Depth

Tattoo inks contain proprietary mixtures of industrial pigments, acrylic binders, and metal oxides. Variable needle penetration depth and carrier chemistry alter fragmentation thresholds.

3. Smoking & Microcirculation

In the published clinical literature, smoking was shown to decrease the likelihood of 10-session tattoo clearance by approximately 70% due to chronic peripheral vasoconstriction.

4. White Ink & Paradoxical Darkening

Cosmetic tattoos and highlight inks containing titanium dioxide (TiO2) or iron oxide can oxidize and turn permanently black or slate grey upon Q-switched laser impact.

  3. 3. Pigment Specifics & Melanin Protection

    What laser wavelengths will you use for my specific ink colours, and what precautions do you take to protect my natural skin melanin from hypopigmentation or hyperpigmentation?

    What is your clinical protocol if the tattoo hits a fading plateau or stalls after several sessions?

  5. 5. Cover-up Collaboration (If Applicable)

    If fading for a cover-up, will you assess the tattoo alongside my tattoo artist to determine the exact point where sufficient lightening has occurred to support new artwork?

Frequently Asked Clinical Questions

Evidence-based answers regarding laser tattoo removal mechanics, preparation, and plateaus.

What happens if laser tattoo removal stops working or hits a fading plateau? #

A fading plateau commonly occurs when the easily fragmented superficial ink has been cleared, leaving deeper, larger, or more stubborn pigment clusters behind. When fading stalls, your provider may re-evaluate their approach for what remains, or extend the interval between sessions to 12 to 16 weeks to give the body more time to clear the fragmented pigment without overworking the skin. What changes, if anything, is a clinical decision made in person by your provider, not something this tool can specify.

How should I prepare my skin before a laser tattoo removal session? #

Optimal preparation protects your skin from adverse pigment changes. Avoid all sun exposure, tanning beds, and self-tanners for at least 4 to 6 weeks prior to treatment, as excess epidermal melanin competes for laser energy and increases hypopigmentation risks. Shave the treatment area 24 hours prior to prevent singed surface hair. Ensure skin is completely clean and free of cosmetics, moisturizers, deodorants, or self-applied numbing creams unless specifically applied under clinical direction. Stay well hydrated and avoid alcohol or aspirin 24 hours before treatment.

Why do multi-colored inks (green, blue, yellow) require different lasers than black? #

Laser tattoo removal works by a pigment absorbing light energy and fracturing into smaller pieces the body can clear; a given wavelength only works well if the target pigment actually absorbs it. Black ink absorbs a very broad range of light and generally responds most reliably. Red, blue, green, and purple pigments absorb more narrowly, and yellow is notoriously the hardest colour to clear because it absorbs light poorly across the range that is safe for skin. This is exactly why multi-colour work often needs more sessions than solid black. Which laser and wavelength your provider chooses for your specific ink is a clinical decision made in person, not something this tool can specify.

Can white ink or cosmetic tattoo pigment turn black when lasered? #

Yes. This phenomenon is known clinically as paradoxical darkening. White ink, flesh tones, and many cosmetic pigments contain titanium dioxide (TiO2) or ferric iron oxide (Fe2O3). Upon laser exposure, high-peak energy reduces titanium and iron oxides into lower oxidation states, instantly turning white, pink, or skin-toned pigment into permanent dark grey or black. A qualified practitioner must always perform a discrete test spot on cosmetic or white ink before proceeding.

How much does smoking tobacco affect the number of sessions required? #

In published clinical studies (such as Kirby et al. and Bencini et al.), smoking was shown to decrease the 10-session clearance rate by approximately 70%. Nicotine causes chronic cutaneous vasoconstriction, which sharply impairs peripheral microcirculation. Because ink removal depends entirely on immune macrophages transporting shattered ink particles through the bloodstream and lymphatic vessels, reduced blood flow significantly prolongs required clearance times.

When is a tattoo faded enough for a cover-up instead of complete clearance? #

A cover-up generally requires only 50% to 70% fading rather than 95%+ complete clearance. This reduces the required sessions by roughly 35% to 45%. You should collaborate with your tattoo artist when you reach the cover-up window: once dark solid outlines have lightened to a soft grey, the artist can place richer, vibrant colors or detailed designs without the old design showing through. Wait 8 to 12 weeks after your last laser session before tattooing to ensure dermal healing is complete.

How does my Fitzpatrick skin type influence laser safety and treatment speed? #

Fitzpatrick skin types I to III have less epidermal melanin, which generally gives a provider more room to work without affecting the surrounding skin colour. Skin types IV to VI contain more natural melanin, which competes with the tattoo pigment for the laser's energy and raises the risk of the skin itself darkening or lightening (hyperpigmentation or hypopigmentation) if the approach is not adjusted for that skin type. A provider experienced with darker skin types will adjust their approach accordingly; this is exactly why the plan is set in person, not by a website.

Does laser tattoo removal hurt, and what pain management options are available? #

Most clients compare the sensation of a laser pulse to the snap of a rubber band against the skin accompanied by intense heat. Because treatment areas are often sensitive, modern clinics use several pain mitigation strategies: continuous forced cold air chilling directed at the skin before, during, and after pulses; topical compounded numbing creams applied 30 to 60 minutes prior; and ice compression. In clinical settings, local infiltration may be offered for highly sensitive anatomical sites.

Why are session intervals spaced 8 to 12+ weeks apart, and can treatments be scheduled sooner? #

Rushing sessions does not speed up tattoo removal and significantly increases the risk of scarring and dyspigmentation. The laser does not eliminate ink directly; it merely shatters large pigment clumps into microscopic granules. Your body's immune macrophages and lymphatic system require at least 8 to 12 weeks to engulf, transport, and excrete these particles. Treating too soon fires laser energy at pigment that the body is still clearing, causing unnecessary tissue trauma with diminished clearing efficiency.
